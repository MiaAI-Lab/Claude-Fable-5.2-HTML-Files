Of the Arithmetic

Take two planets and a ruler. Every few days join them with a straight line, then let them move on. Nothing else is done here. Venus completes her circuit of the Sun in 224.70 days, the Earth in 365.26, and the quotient of those numbers, 1.6256, lies very near to thirteen eighths. So while the Earth goes round eight times Venus goes round thirteen, overtaking us five times on the way, at five places set evenly about the circle. Each line falls close beside the last; where they cross, their envelope shows as a curve, and the curve has five petals.

No influence passes between the two worlds to arrange this. The rose is a fact of division, as plain as a remainder. Change the pair and the fraction changes, and with it the count of loops, which is always the difference of the two terms. Nor is the fit exact: thirteen circuits of Venus fall about a day short of eight years, so each rose lies a hair askew of the last, and none is traced twice. That a sum should prove so handsome is no reason to distrust the sum.
