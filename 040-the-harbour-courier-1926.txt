TITLE
The Harbour Courier — Tuesday, 9 March 1926

PROMPT
The front page of a broadsheet newspaper from an invented port city, "Saint Elmo's Bay", dated Tuesday 9 March 1926, typeset with the density and craft of real letterpress newspapers: nameplate, ears, multi-deck headlines, column rules, justified text, cross-heads, small ads, a weather box, shipping intelligence, and procedurally generated halftone "photographs". Every story is invented, internally consistent and written in period voice. Concept: an editorial page that is all typography and ink; nothing but black on newsprint.

Mood: bustling, civic, faintly comic, convincingly old. Palette: newsprint #EDE6D6 (with subtle yellowing toward the edges and a fold line across the middle), ink black #151515, and a worn grey #6B6B6B for halftones and lighter rules. The printed sheet is strictly black and white; the only colour anywhere is the brass rim of the loupe laid upon it. Texture: paper grain, slight ink spread, occasional uneven impression (very subtle per-column opacity variation), show-through of reversed type from the other side at ~3% opacity.

Typography (system fonts only, used with skill): nameplate "The Harbour Courier" in a blackletter-flavoured treatment drawn as SVG lettering or, failing that, a heavily styled old-style serif with ornamental rules, an engraved harbour vignette (lighthouse, steamer, gulls) in SVG between the words, ears either side (weather: "Fresh to strong S.W. winds; showers; glass falling" and price: "ONE PENNY"), a dateline rule with "No. 14,902 · ESTABLISHED 1841 · LATE CITY EDITION". Headlines in condensed bold capitals with multiple decks decreasing in size and alternating caps/italic, separated by short centred dashes; body in a tight book serif (Georgia/"Times New Roman") at small size, fully justified with hyphenation enabled, first paragraph in slightly larger type, first words in caps; column rules as hairlines.

Layout: seven columns on desktop in a true broadsheet grid with stories spanning different column counts, turning to three columns on tablets and a single column on phones (reflowed as a narrow "late extra" edition, still dense and elegant). Content: lead story across three columns: "GREAT FOG HALTS THE PORT — Forty Vessels at Anchor in the Roads — FERRY STRIKES BUOY; NO LIVES LOST — Harbour Master's Remarkable Statement"; second lead: the municipal tramway's new electric line and a ratepayers' protest; a human-interest piece about an escaped circus sea-lion seen at the fish market; "SHIPPING INTELLIGENCE" as a tight tabular list of arrivals and sailings with invented vessels, tonnages and cargoes; "THE WEATHER" box with a tiny isobar chart drawn in SVG; a serial fiction instalment ("The Lamp at Sorrel Point — Chapter XIV"); cricket and football results from invented clubs; a court report; a column of classified advertisements with period phrasing (lost umbrellas, a parrot that speaks Portuguese, piano lessons), and two two-column display advertisements with engraved-style SVG illustrations ("Dr. Fenwick's Marine Tonic", "Hallam's Waterproofs — For Men Who Go Down to the Sea"), plus small boxed house notices that square off the columns. Roughly 2,400 words of genuinely readable copy (both editions included), cross-referenced between stories, court report and small ads.

Halftone photographs (signature moment): two or three "press photographs" generated on canvas: a procedural grey-scale scene (a steamer looming in fog beside a quay with cranes; a crowd of hats at the tram depot; the sea-lion on a fish slab) rendered through a real halftone screen: a 45° grid of black dots whose radius encodes tone, with slight dot gain and misalignment. On load the page is "printed": a press cylinder shadow sweeps down the sheet and ink arrives column by column over ~2 seconds. Hovering or tapping a photograph brings a brass-rimmed loupe that magnifies the halftone dots under the cursor (drawn at 4×), revealing the screen; the caption sits beneath in italic with a credit ("Courier photograph").

Interaction: an "editions" control styled as a small boxed notice ("STOP PRESS — pull the 4 a.m. Extra") that reprints the page with a press-run animation and changes the lead headline and decks ("FOG LIFTING; PORT AWAKES"), the dateline edition label, the fog story's latest paragraph, the shipping note and a late-news fudge box in the margin ("FOG LIFTING — pilot cutter reports the Roads clearing"); clicking any headline folds the paper to enlarge that story for comfortable reading and back; hovering small ads underlines them like a pencil mark and one can be "circled" in pencil on click. Keyboard accessible with focus outlines styled as pencil boxes. Reduced motion: no press sweep; edition changes cross-fade.

DESCRIPTION
A complete broadsheet front page for an invented port city, printed in ink black on yellowing newsprint: a blackletter nameplate with an engraved harbour vignette, weather and price ears, a ruled dateline, and seven columns of period journalism about a great fog, a new electric tramway, an escaped circus sea-lion, a police court, a serial story, shipping, sport and small advertisements whose details quietly refer to one another. On arrival a press cylinder's shadow rolls down the sheet and the ink lands block by block; the three press photographs are true halftones that can be examined dot by dot under a brass loupe. The paper carries grain, a centre fold, uneven impression and the ghost of page two showing through, and it reflows to three columns on tablets and a dense single-column "late extra" on phones.

VISUAL TECHNIQUES
- Blackletter nameplate generated as SVG by sweeping a broad-nib pen (fixed 38° angle) along hand-plotted stroke skeletons, giving authentic thick and thin strokes
- Hand-drawn inline SVG engravings: harbour vignette with lighthouse, steamer and gulls; isobar chart; tonic bottle; sou'westered fisherman; printer's fist
- Procedural greyscale scenes painted on an offscreen canvas (fog-bound steamer and crane, hats at the tram depot, sea-lion on a marble slab) and converted to a real 45° amplitude-modulated halftone screen with dot gain, jittered registration and uneven impression
- Loupe canvas that re-screens the photograph at 4x with rough-edged dots and paper fibres, inside a conic-gradient brass rim
- Condensed headline type made from bold serif capitals compressed horizontally, sized with container query units; multi-deck headlines alternating capitals and italics with centred dashes
- CSS grid broadsheet layout with hairline column rules, multi-column justified body text with soft hyphens, cross-heads kept with their paragraphs, dotted leaders in tabular lists
- Paper texture from a generated grain tile, edge yellowing gradients, horizontal and vertical fold creases, mirrored show-through type at about 3% opacity, per-column ink opacity and a faint ink-spread shadow
- Press-run animation: a cylinder shadow sweeps the viewport while each block is revealed by a clip-path wipe timed to its position, staggered left to right
- Hinged 3D unfold of a newsprint reading panel with its own crease
- Pencil underline drawn across wrapped lines with an animated background, and hand-wobbled SVG rings drawn with stroke-dash animation

INTERACTION MODEL
Hovering a photograph (or tapping it, or focusing it and using the arrow keys) brings up the brass loupe, which magnifies the halftone dots under the pointer four times; moving away, tapping elsewhere, scrolling or Escape puts it down. The boxed "STOP PRESS" notice reprints the sheet as the 4 a.m. Extra with a fresh press run, changing the lead headline and decks, the edition label, the latest fog paragraph, the shipping note and the stop-press box; pressing it again restores the Late City Edition. Clicking any story headline folds a newsprint panel out over the page with that story set large for reading, its photograph re-screened and still explorable; "Fold back", Escape or a click outside returns to the sheet. Small advertisements gain a pencil underline on hover or focus and are ringed in pencil on click, and unringed on a second click. Every control is reachable by keyboard with pencil-box focus outlines; with reduced motion the press sweep and unfold are removed and editions cross-fade.
