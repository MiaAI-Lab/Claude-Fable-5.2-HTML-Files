Joinery

Detail A — Head lug

Top tube to head tube · silver brazed
  1. 1
    Feather window, pierced by hand.It lets me watch the silver run right through the joint. That it looks like a kestrel's primary is the reason I chose the shape, not the reason for the hole.
  2. 2
    Long point, filed thin at the tip.A lug that stops dead makes a hard spot in a thin tube. A thinned point lets the stiffness fade out gradually.
  3. 3
    Silver, not brass, on the main tubes.It flows at a much lower heat, so the butted ends keep the strength the mill put into them.

Detail B — Bottom bracket

Fillet brazed · filed by hand
  1. 1
    Brass fillet, laid in three passes.Then filed until the tubes look as though they grew out of one another. About four hours a shell, and my favourite four.
  2. 2
    Threaded 68 mm shell, English thread.Any decent bike shop on earth can service it with tools they already own. Nothing pressed, nothing creaking.
  3. 3
    No lug here, on purpose.A cast shell fixes the stay angles. A fillet lets me put the chainstays exactly where your tyres and heels need them. See Sheet 2.

Detail C — Rear dropout

Stainless plate · stays slotted & domed
  1. 1
    Vertical slot.The wheel can only sit in one place, so brakes and mudguard lines stay true after every puncture, even one mended in the dark.
  2. 2
    Stay ends slotted, domed and brazed shut.A square-cut end holds water and starts to rust from the inside. A dome sheds it.
  3. 3
    Two eyelets each side.One for the mudguard stay, one for the rack. In the Calder valley neither is optional.
