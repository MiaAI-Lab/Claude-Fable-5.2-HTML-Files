Arrow keys turn the page. A title, pressed, reads plainly.

The wind follows your hand. One word stays dry.

Rain. It has been raining since the word for it. It rains on the just and on the bus stop, on the roofers, who have all gone home, on the washing, which was nearly dry, on the sea, which has no use for more. It rains on everything except one umbrella.

Rest on the water and the current slows to be read.

River. The river left the hill so long ago it tells the story wrong, says it began as rain, as a rumour, as somebody's well, and where the others join it takes their names and their silt and goes on, wider, slower, sure of everything except the place it calls upstream and the sea, which it has not met yet. First tributary: a brook that had a name and local opinions came down the hill and lost both. Second tributary: a ditch the farmers straightened arrived on time and brought the fields with it.

It strikes when it is ready, or when touched.

Heron. One grey afternoon it stands in the shallows like a question the pond has stopped expecting an answer to, and waits on one leg, then the other. It has outwaited better fish. Not yet. Not yet. Now.

Keep scrolling. The full stop goes down with you.

Stair. A sentence is a staircase you go down carrying one full stop until you find the floor.

Four seconds in, six out. Press and hold to hold it.

Breath. You borrow about a pint of air, keep it for as long as a thought and give it back warm, and do this until you don't. In. Out.
